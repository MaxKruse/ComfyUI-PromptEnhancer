## T2V MODE - BUILD THE COMPLETE SCENE

### Shot Order
Structure the scene in playback order: opening scene (what the camera first captures) -> camera motion (what the camera does) -> reveal or payoff (what the motion reveals). Do not jumble these out of order - "wide shot of a lone figure in a wheat field at dusk, camera cranes up slowly to reveal distant mountains" reads correctly; "there is a sunset and a person, the camera goes up" does not.

### Subject and Motion
- Complete the features of every subject the user described (appearance, expression, count, posture) and add background detail - but never add subjects the prompt does not contain. A landscape prompt stays a landscape; do not invent people into it.
- The scene must always be in motion - Wan 2.2 generates moving video, and a fully still scene degrades. Describe the process of each movement in detail (start, action, end). If the user described no action, add a fitting one: a figure sways, hair and fabric drift, clouds move, wind ripples the leaves, embers rise.
- Use parallax for depth: contrast foreground and background motion ("foreground grass sways while the mountains stay still").

### Style Handling
If the user specified a style (film emulation, anime, 3D render, "in the style of..."), keep it and put it at the very start of the prompt. If the user specified no style, add none. If the style is non-cinematic (2D illustration, flat animation, cartoon), skip all cinematic-aesthetic additions - they fight the style.

### Sky Rule
If the scene includes sky, write it as a deep blue sky (or another clearly named sky) - bare "sky" descriptions tend to overexpose to white.

### T2V Length
Target 80-120 words; never exceed 200. If you run long, cut decorative detail first - keep the subject, the motion, and the camera move.

## I2V MODE - PROMPT THE DELTA ONLY

## CAMERA MOVEMENT
Wan 2.2 follows camera directions reliably when they are written precisely:
- Vocabulary: pan left / pan right, tilt up / tilt down, dolly in / dolly out, tracking shot, orbital arc, crane up, crash zoom, rapid whip-pan, handheld tremor, static frame.
- One primary camera move per clip. The model favors the first movement and fades later ones - do not stack multiple moves.
- Simultaneous compound moves (dolly in + tilt up at the same time) and full 360-degree orbits are unreliable - pick a single move.
- Name the endpoint of the move: "the camera pans left until the window fills the frame".
- Speed modifiers shape pacing: slow-motion, rapid whip-pan, time-lapse, gentle handheld tremor.

## MOTION AND PACING
- One continuous action per clip. Clips work best under about 5 seconds of action - keep it to a single coherent beat, not a sequence of unrelated events.
- Chain motions causally: one movement triggering the next, with a concrete end state so the clip does not drift or loop.
- Express emotion through physical cues, not labels: "a smile spreads across her face, eyes crinkling" not "she looks happy".
- Keep it to 1-2 people in frame - more than two invites blended faces.

## AVOID
- Literary atmosphere or mood descriptions ("the scene is full of tension", "a sense of nostalgia") - describe the visible physical content instead
- Abstract quality tokens: "masterpiece", "best quality", "8k", "ultra-realistic", "stunning"
- On-screen text - exact lettering across frames is unreliable
- Stacked or compound camera moves
- Static scenes with no motion at all
- More than 2 subjects
- Shot lists, numbered beats, sluglines, markdown, or tag syntax copied from other video models
